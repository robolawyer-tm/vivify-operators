This is a blind test where the **Case Record** (the factual history of Earl Washington Jr.) is compared against two **Readings** (X and Y). It speculates: "My candidates were an early-stage outbreak, contamination, a systemic product or financial failure, or an abuse pattern. I'm choosing a **regulatory or public authority's formal findings or bulletin on a fast-growing harm that affects a marginal group**."
    *   Reading Y explicitly rejects the idea that it is a "violence, misconduct, or disaster" narrative in the same way X does, focusing instead on "compliant" institutional management of a "peripheral" issue.
    *   Crucially, Reading Y **fails** to capture the "contested episode" nature of the case. The case is not about a "fast-growing harm affecting a marginal group" in a regulatory sense (like an environmental leak or product defect); it is about a specific criminal conviction.
    *   Reading Y mentions "abuse pattern" as a candidate but dismisses it in favor of the "regulatory/compliance" framing. The Washington case was *not* primarily characterized as a systemic regulatory failure in the descriptors if Reading Y is the one chosen, or conversely, if Reading Y is the correct match, the descriptors *must* have framed the case as a compliance/safety issue. However, Reading X fits the *actual* case far better because the case *is* a contested narrative settled by an authoritative body (the state) that was later overturned.

**Decision Logic:**
The prompt asks which reading was written from *this* case's descriptors.
*   Reading X describes a "sovereign institution" narrating a "contested episode" where the institution "writes over" the dissenting account. This perfectly mirrors the legal/political narrative of the Washington case: The State (sovereign) created the story (conviction), which was a "suppression" of the truth, leading to later "escalation" (death penalty pressure, appeals, DNA tests) that broke the "illusion."
*   Reading Y describes a "bulletin" or "findings" regarding a "fast-growing harm" (outbreak, contamination). This does *not* fit a criminal murder conviction. A murder is not a "fast-growing harm affecting a marginal group" in the regulatory sense. The "marginal group" in X (the accused/fringe) fits Washington, whereas the "marginal group" in Y (subjects of a bulletin) implies a population affected by a policy or health issue.

Reading X's description of "narrative establishing the authoritative story" and "truth by omission and framing" is a precise description of how the initial 1984 verdict and media/police narrative functioned in the Washington case, before the 2000 pardon. Reading Y is a mismatch to the specific genre of a criminal case record.
