# Round-trip decode

## 1. The reading

**The situation I think this is:** a small voluntary group puts out an announcement to its members. It tells the story of something that happened and slips in guidance about how things will work from now on. The group could be a club, a collective, a small online community, a practice circle or a tight-knit association. Its authority rests on something the text never names.

**Who is involved.** The actors are a small group, not an institution. Members join and leave individually (grid 0.35, group 0.25, individualist). They are not a hierarchy and not a tightly bound sect. Yet the group talks in bureaucratic, contractual language, the way small groups do when they write themselves bylaws, codes of conduct or "how we do things here" documents to feel more solid than they are. The speaker speaks for the group (authority: group), and the society overlay means the group sees itself against wider social norms.

**What happened structurally.** This is a sincere factual account (assertive, cooperative honored) sent one-to-many to the whole membership (broadcast). It is mainly a story with an instruction folded in: "here is what happened, and so here is what we expect." It will be remembered as an event ("remember when the group posted about X") rather than absorbed as ritual or doctrine (episodic memory). It persists because it gets written down and kept (durable).

**The odd feature: occult authority.** The claim's legitimacy comes from somewhere hidden or unstated. Examples: "it has been decided," "the spirit of the community," an anonymous committee, tradition nobody wrote down, or a literally esoteric or spiritual source. I'm choosing between two pictures:
- **(a) Hidden decision-maker.** A mundane group, where the announcement's real author or decision process is kept out of view.
- **(b) Esoteric practice group.** A spiritual or magical group whose guidance is framed as coming from a non-human source.

The contractual language with bureaucratic density tips me slightly toward (a). With the lower-confidence alternative in mind, I'd still name (b) as live.

**How it felt.** Mostly calm. Resonance is harmony, so the audience largely accepts the story and the message lands without friction. Anyone paying attention might notice that it is unclear who actually decided this.

**Conflict shape.** The conflict is latent, early and still forming. The move is *positioning* on the *center* ground: the speaker frames events and norms before any dispute takes shape. It is a pre-emptive narrative, not a response to open disagreement.

**Likely resolution.** Most likely nothing visible happens: the account becomes the accepted version. The failure mode is later. If someone asks "who decided this?", the hidden authority becomes the fault line, and the calm acceptance turns out to have been deferred conflict rather than resolved conflict.

**Internal inconsistency, and it matters for the test.** The conflict block's `schema_signals` don't match the logos coordinates they supposedly derive from:

| Signal | Conflict block says | Logos block says |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | `sovereign` | `occult` |
| Structural layer | `institution` | `small_group` |
| Grid | `0.85` (hierarchical) | `0.35` |
| Resonance | `illusion` | `harmony` |

- **Taken alone, the conflict signals describe a different situation:** a hierarchical institution with sovereign authority broadcasting a narrative the audience mistakes for reality. That is closer to state or corporate messaging.
- **Only the behavior signals line up with both blocks:** `utility:narrative` and `act_type:assertive`.
- **What this points to:** the conflict layer was probably computed from a different record, or the record was shuffled or spliced. If this is the shuffled condition, the mismatch is the detectable seam.
- **How I resolved it:** my reading follows the logos block, which is internally coherent. The conflict labels (latent, positioning, center, forming, early) are loose enough to fit either picture.

## 2. The residue (what the coordinates can't tell you)

- **Content:** what the story actually said, what happened, and what the instruction was.
- **Domain:** hobby club, religious circle, workplace team, online forum or activist cell are all compatible.
- **Identity of the authority:** "occult" says the source is hidden, not *what* it is, or whether hiding it is deliberate, accidental or sacred.
- **Who the speaker is:** founder, moderator, secretary, or an anonymous voice.
- **The audience:** size, makeup, and whether any dissent existed below the "harmony" reading.
- **What the conflict is about:** latent over what, and who is on the other side of the forming window.
- **Time and place:** era, culture, language, medium (letter, post, meeting minutes, sermon).
- **Tone and emotion:** warmth, anxiety, defensiveness, humor. The coordinates record structure, not feeling.
- **What happened next:** the encoding captures one act, so the resolution is inferred, not observed.
- **Which conflict reading is true:** the logos/conflict mismatch means I can't tell which of the two situations the record actually came from.
