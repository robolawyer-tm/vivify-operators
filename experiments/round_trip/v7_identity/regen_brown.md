## 1. The Reading

This is most plausibly a **state's formal, on-the-record statement of fact to an international audience**, built on figures its own agencies produced and nobody independently checked, and rounded upward. My concrete picture is a national government's official submission or published report to a multilateral body. Examples would be a progress report on a treaty commitment, a national statistics release, or a white paper on development, emissions or security outcomes. It is entered into the permanent record and meant to be cited.

**The actors and the act.** The speaker holds sovereign authority, either a government or a ministry speaking for one. The act is an assertion: "this is how things stand." The setting is strongly hierarchical, with high grid and moderate group. The statement comes out through formal channels, in formal language, with clear lines of who may speak for whom. It is not personal and not improvised. It reads like an official document in the formal register used for a treaty body, a statistical yearbook or a parliamentary record.

**The weak point.** The account behind the statement was supplied by the institution itself, shaped by no outside party, and inflated. That combination matters. There was no adversarial check, no auditor, and probably no deliberate fabricator. "Shaped by nobody" suggests the inflation was not one person's lie. It is more likely systemic: reporting incentives at each level, generous definitions and optimistic aggregation, each step defensible on its own. This also explains why the cooperative norms are marked as *honored*. On its face the statement is orderly, relevant and sincerely delivered. The speaker probably believes it, or at least is not knowingly lying. The flaw sits upstream, in the account, not in the act of speaking.

**What the statement is for.** Its main function is narrative: it tells a story of competence, progress or compliance. Its secondary function is currency: the claim is something to spend. It can buy standing in negotiations, access to funding, credit toward commitments, or relief from scrutiny. Because it is archived and durable, it becomes a reference point that others will build on. That is exactly what makes the inflation costly later.

**The conflict.** The statement lands with friction rather than acceptance. Outside observers start to notice that the numbers don't quite fit. These might be other states, NGOs, researchers, journalists or the receiving body's technical staff. The conflict is early, and the schema is activated: a known pattern ("official figures from this source run high") has been triggered. The institution responds by rationalizing. It explains the methodology, cites definitional differences, points to data limitations and restates the figures in more careful language. It neither retracts nor escalates.

**How it feels inside.** For the officials, it probably feels like routine, careful work that is now being unfairly questioned. They followed procedure, and the numbers came up the chain. For the skeptics, it feels like a polished, well-defended surface that is hard to get purchase on. They sense the inflation but face a formally correct document and a sovereign speaker who cannot easily be compelled.

**Likely resolution.** The terrain is drifting, so a clean resolution is unlikely. The most plausible path is slow erosion rather than confrontation. Footnoted revisions appear in later editions. Independent estimates diverge and are cited alongside the official figure. The original claim gradually loses value as currency while staying in the archive as "the official number." A sharper break is possible only if an external shock forces a reckoning, such as an audit, a leak or a visible outcome that contradicts the claim. At this early, rationalizing stage, the default is quiet persistence with growing discount.

**Where I chose among alternatives:**
- A central bank or major intergovernmental organization publishing its own self-assessment would fit almost as well.
- A sovereign's official historical or casualty account would also fit the "narrative plus archive" profile.
- I leaned toward a statistical or progress submission because the secondary "currency" function fits figures that are traded for credit or standing.
- "Global" scale suggests the audience is international rather than domestic.

## 2. The Residue

The coordinates cannot recover:

- **Identity:** which state or institution, which body received the statement, and which country, era or policy domain.
- **The content of the claim:** what was asserted, which figures, and in what units.
- **The size and mechanism of the inflation:** whether it was modest rounding or gross overstatement, whether it came from definitions, sampling, incentives or deliberate padding, and at what level it entered.
- **Intent and knowledge:** whether senior officials knew, suspected or genuinely believed the account. "Shaped by nobody" rules out a single author, but it does not settle how much anyone knew.
- **Who is pushing back:** the specific challengers, their power, and their evidence.
- **Stakes:** what the "currency" actually buys, such as money, votes, treaty credit or prestige, and how much.
- **Timeline and outcome:** how long the drift lasts, whether the claim was later corrected, and any actual consequences.
- **Texture:** tone, wording, rhetorical moves, the personalities of the officials, and the emotional temperature beyond what the structure implies.
- **Domestic dimension:** whether the same statement plays differently at home, and whether internal politics drive the inflation.
- **Counterfactual baseline:** what the true figure was, or whether anyone could know it.
