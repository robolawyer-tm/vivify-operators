## 1. The Reading

This is most plausibly an **official written finding from a state-backed body**: a report, determination, or formal statement entered into the record. It states as settled fact something that rests on a real but thin foundation. The body's own experts or officials took a modest piece of evidence, such as a statistical figure, a technical assurance, or a cluster of weak indicators, and presented it as near-certain. Nobody downstream reworked or challenged it. It was relied on as delivered.

**The actors.** On one side is an institution speaking with sovereign weight: a ministry, regulator, prosecutorial or forensic service, or a public body acting in the state's name. Its language is contractual and procedural, the register of findings, obligations, and determinations rather than persuasion. On the other side are people who sit under that institution's rules but are not organized together. They are high-grid, low-group "isolates," each facing the system alone. Examples would be individual claimants, defendants, licensees, small operators, or employees, each assuming their case is unique.

**Where I choose among alternatives.** The inflated account could be a forensic or scientific claim used in legal proceedings, or a technical or system-reliability assurance used in administrative enforcement. I lean toward the second: an institution vouching for its own system or method ("the data is reliable," "the error rate is negligible") and then acting against individuals on that basis. The narrative purpose, with instruction as a secondary purpose, fits a document that tells the official story of what happened and also directs what should follow from it.

**Why it is both sincere and false.** The communicative act appears honest: the speakers believe what they assert and do not obviously breach conversational norms. Yet the effect is illusory, because it lands as authoritative truth while resting on an overstated base. This is not a lie in the ordinary sense. It is institutional overconfidence that has hardened into record. The archive channel matters here. Once written down, the claim becomes the institution's memory and is cited forward, gaining authority with each reuse.

**The conflict.** The institution's credibility, liability, and procedures are bound up with the claim, so the conflict is entangled: admitting weakness in the account means admitting harm done on its basis. Its behavior is suppression. Challenges are dismissed, contrary evidence is discounted, and challengers are treated as cranks or as self-interested. Those challengers occupy fringe terrain: scattered individuals, a few persistent advocates, perhaps a niche journalist or specialist, all far from the centers of decision.

The institution is in a rationalizing window. It is past the point of simple error and is now producing justifications for decisions already made. The escalation is exponential, not linear. Each new case decided on the inflated account adds to the stack, every new case raises the cost of reversal, and isolated complaints begin to multiply and eventually link up.

**How it felt.** For those affected, it likely felt bewildering and lonely: being told by an unquestionable authority that the record proves something you know to be untrue, with no peer group to confirm you are not alone. Inside the institution, it likely felt routine and defensible, a matter of following procedure and trusting one's own experts.

**Likely resolution.** Situations shaped this way rarely resolve from within. The most plausible arc is a long period of suppression, followed by a break from outside: isolates finding each other, litigation, an external expert review, or press exposure. That break is typically followed by a belated official reckoning. At the moment captured, though, the matter is unresolved and still accelerating.

## 2. The Residue

The coordinates cannot tell me:

- **Domain and setting:** which country, era, or sector (justice, welfare, health, finance, technology).
- **The actual claim:** what was asserted, what the weak underlying element was, and how far it was inflated.
- **Who the individuals are:** how many there were, their circumstances, and what each one lost (money, liberty, livelihood, reputation, life).
- **Internal knowledge:** whether anyone inside the institution doubted the account, raised concerns, or was overruled, and whether the overconfidence was naïve or knowing.
- **Specific suppression tactics:** whether through legal process, non-disclosure, intimidation, or simple bureaucratic inertia.
- **The original wording:** the exact language, tone, and rhetorical moves of the text.
- **Outcome and timing:** whether, when, and how the account was ever corrected, and whether there was accountability or redress.
- **Outside actors:** the role of media, courts, legislators, or advocacy groups.
- **Human texture:** the emotional and personal dimension of particular lives, which no structural encoding preserves.
