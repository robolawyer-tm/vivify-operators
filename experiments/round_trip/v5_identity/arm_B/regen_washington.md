## 1. The Reading

**The most plausible picture:** a state body, or an organ acting with state-level authority, issues a formal public statement justifying action it has taken against a person or small group on the margins of society. The statement rests on an account the target supplied, most likely a confession, a signed admission, or a "voluntary" statement. The institution manufactured the conditions under which that account was produced and shaped its wording.

**What happened structurally.** The communicative act is a declaration of fact ("X did this; X has admitted it"), delivered with sovereign authority to a general audience. It is written in bureaucratic, contractual language: references to procedures, articles, signed documents and findings. It aims to tell a story (what happened, who is responsible) and secondarily to instruct the public on how to understand it and what not to do. The hierarchical setting means the speaker's ranking does the persuading. The target belongs to the system, as a citizen, member or employee, but has no standing within it to contest the account.

**The core mechanism.** The institution's case relies on the target's own words, extracted or scripted by the institution. This is the classic televised confession, or the dismissed insider made to sign a statement admitting misconduct. It explains why the statement reads as cooperative. It is orderly, relevant and appears truthful on its face, so it honors conversational norms. Yet what it produces is an illusion: an appearance of consensus and legitimacy whose foundation was produced by the party asserting it. Here the coordinates pull in different directions, and I read them together as follows: the surface is cooperative, and the deception lies in where the evidence came from, not in how the sentences are built.

**The shape of the conflict.** The conflict is entangled. Target and institution are bound together through membership, employment, citizenship or shared history, so the target cannot simply leave, and the institution cannot simply ignore the target. The behavior is suppression aimed at a fringe actor, such as a dissident, a minority-movement figure, or a whistleblower without an organized constituency. The statement arrives in the rationalizing window, after the action has been taken, to supply its justification. Meanwhile escalation is exponential. The justification is being issued while things are speeding up: more detentions, more pressure, wider circles of suspicion, or growing outside attention that the institution feels it must answer. I read the statement as an attempt to impose a settled narrative on a situation that is getting away from the institution.

**How it felt from inside.** For the target, there is the particular humiliation of hearing one's own voice used as the weapon. They are cut off from rebuttal, and their associates may hesitate between believing the confession and believing it was coerced. For the target's fringe community, the instructional layer reads as a warning. For the wider public, the account is smooth and official enough to be plausible, and most people accept it or look away. For the people inside the institution writing the statement, it is probably routine paperwork.

**Likely resolution.** In the short run, the institution's account probably becomes the official record. It is stored in institutional memory and repeated, and the suppression succeeds formally. Exponential escalation, though, suggests it does not stay settled. The more the institution has to rationalize, the more it reveals that the matter is contested. Later retractions, outside reporting or the target's eventual counter-account may break the illusion, but usually long after the practical consequences have landed.

**Alternatives I rejected.** The setting could be a large corporation or a religious or party organization rather than a state, announcing a member's expulsion based on a signed admission. The "sovereign" authority and the society-wide overlay make a state or state-equivalent actor more likely, so I chose that, though an organization with sovereign-like power over its members fits almost as well.

## 2. The Residue

The coordinates cannot tell me:

- **Identity and place:** who the institution and the target are, the country or organization, the era, and the political system.
- **Content:** what the target allegedly did, what the confession or statement actually says, and the exact wording of the public announcement.
- **Ground truth:** whether the underlying allegation is partly true, wholly fabricated, or true but extracted unjustly. "Manufactured" describes how the account was produced, not whether it is false.
- **How the account was manufactured:** physical coercion, legal threats, plea-bargain pressure, scripted interviews, editing, or a form with predetermined answers.
- **The target's perspective:** their voice, intent, later recantation, and emotional state.
- **Scale and numbers:** whether one person or many are involved, how fast "exponential" means in practice, and what the triggering escalation was.
- **Audience reception:** whether the public believed it, whether foreign or independent observers challenged it, and how the target's community responded.
- **Medium and channel:** press release, state television, court filing or internal bulletin.
- **Outcome:** sentencing, release, rehabilitation, reversal, or historical reassessment.
- **Texture:** tone, rhetoric, the individual officials involved, and any irony, hedging or internal dissent within the institution.
