**Case Analysis (Josiah Sutton):**
*   **Core Event:** A wrongful conviction based on a flawed DNA analysis performed by the Houston Police Crime Lab (the institution).
*   **The Error:** The lab used outdated/incorrect methods (early PCR) and produced a false "match" while excluding the true perpetrator and co-accused. The lab also made a clerical error regarding "Sample #1" (the car sample), claiming a match when it was an exclusion, and the prosecutor suppressed this information.
*   **The Institutional Act:** The state issued a formal legal judgment (conviction and sentence) based on this faulty internal evidence. The appellate courts upheld it, treating the lab's word as final fact.
*   **The "Record":** The court record and lab reports represented the "official truth." They were internally consistent to the court (the match was presented as definite) but disconnected from biological reality.
*   **Resolution:** The truth emerged not through an internal self-correction or a "confession" from the state, but through external investigation (TV reporters) and independent re-testing which revealed the lab's incompetence. The institution (via the Governor) eventually granted a pardon based on innocence.
*   **Key Dynamic:** The individual (Sutton) was pitted against the state's authoritative systems. He had no power to challenge the "system" (the lab) because that system was the source of the accusation. The "suppression" was the withholding of the conflicting data (the exclusion in Sample #1) and the inability of the defense to challenge the forensic science adequately.
