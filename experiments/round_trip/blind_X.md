# THE READING

**What it is:** A formal, official account issued by an institution with sovereign-level authority, most likely a state body. The best candidates are a government commission report, an agency's official findings, a court or tribunal judgment, or a ministry white paper. It is written to go into the record. It mainly tells a story ("here is what happened and why") and secondarily tells people what to do ("therefore these measures follow"). I'm choosing **an official inquiry or government report that explains a contested event or policy after the fact** over the alternatives, such as a statute, a press release or a founding charter. The archive transmission, the rationalizing window and the narrative-first utility all point to a retrospective, documentary text rather than a live command.

**The structural situation:** The setting is hierarchical: grid 0.85 means strong rules, ranks and procedure. Group is only 0.4, so the people inside are held together by position and procedure, not by solidarity or shared identity. The text is bureaucratic and formalized. It speaks as "the system", not as a person, and it assumes it will be kept on file. A society-wide overlay means its claims reach beyond the institution into public understanding.

**The central tension:** The communication follows the cooperative rules. It is relevant, orderly and probably accurate sentence by sentence, and no maxim is broken. Yet its resonance is *illusion*. The distortion isn't in lies. It is in the framing: what gets told as the story, what gets left out as outside scope, and the sense of closure and coherence the official form produces. That fits the conflict profile:
- **Entangled schema:** the institution is both a party to the situation and the one who writes it down. Authority, structure and hierarchy are wound together, so the account can't be separated from the power that produced it.
- **Suppression:** the conflict is handled by crowding out competing accounts, not by confronting them. The official story takes up the space where other versions would go.
- **Fringe terrain:** what is being suppressed sits at the margins. It might be a marginal population, dissenting witnesses, minority experts, or an uncomfortable finding placed in an annex.
- **Rationalizing window with exponential escalation:** this is the most revealing pairing. The document justifies something while the underlying problem is speeding up. The rationalization trails the escalation. It is written in the calm, measured voice of settled procedure about a situation that is not settled and is getting worse fast.

**How it likely felt:** For the authors, it probably felt like routine duty: careful, defensible, even conscientious work. For general readers, it likely felt reassuring and authoritative, the sense that someone competent has looked into it. For the people at the fringe, it likely felt like erasure. Their experience is technically acknowledged, or simply absent, inside a document that becomes the official memory. The sharpest harm is that there is nothing obvious to object to. It is hard to fight a text that follows every rule.

**How it resolves:** For now it doesn't resolve. It stabilizes. The archive keeps the institution's version as the record of record, and the suppressed account stays at the margins. Exponential escalation suggests the gap between the official story and reality widens until it can't be covered over. When it finally breaks, it will most likely be through outside pressure: a counter-report, a later inquiry, journalists or historians reading the archive against the grain. The record then becomes evidence of what it left out.

# THE RESIDUE

The coordinates lose:

1. **Who and where:** the country, the era, the institution, the named authors. It could be a colonial administration's annual report or a 2020s regulator.
2. **The subject matter:** whether this concerns a policing incident, a public-health crisis, an industrial disaster, a war, a financial collapse or something else.
3. **What was suppressed:** the actual content of the fringe account, and whose it was.
4. **Truth values:** whether the illusion comes from deliberate strategy, institutional blind spots or honest limits of method. "Cooperative honored" plus "illusion" can't tell cynicism from sincerity.
5. **Which instructions:** what the secondary "instruction" function actually directs, and who has to comply.
6. **What is escalating:** exponential growth in what (casualties, costs, unrest, spread), and over what time span.
7. **Voice and texture:** tone, rhetorical moves, hedges, specific wording. These are exactly where framing effects live, and they are flattened here.
8. **Reception and aftermath:** whether anyone challenged it, whether it was later discredited, and what happened to the people at the margins.
9. **Whether the fringe is the subject or the audience:** "fringe terrain" could mean the conflict concerns marginal people, or that the text circulates only in marginal venues.
10. **Genre boundaries:** report vs. judgment vs. white paper vs. official history. These carry different legal force and different chances of being reopened.
11. **The emotional stakes for specific people:** grief, fear or anger. The coordinates encode structure, not suffering.
